Changes and known limitations

Current release: Version 0.19.8 — 22 October 2026. This page gives a simple, user-facing release history without requiring access to GitHub.

0.19.8 — The social value ratio is no longer called an SROI

  • The scorecard row formerly labelled “SROI Ratio” is now “Gross Social Value Multiple”.
  • It is the undiscounted sum of monetised DALYs, time saved and carbon divided by initial grant plus loan capital. It applies no deadweight, attribution, displacement or drop-off, so it is not a formal SROI.
  • The tooltip, clipboard report, CSV export and methodology all say so and list the missing adjustments.
  • No generic adjustment percentages were invented; a formal SROI needs programme evidence for each one.
  • A robustness review registered eight further findings (F-66 to F-73) and four open questions; the most important is that the solvency verdict currently tests both ledgers together although grant cash cannot pay operations. These are documented, not yet fixed.
  • F-65 is resolved. Numeric results and all 21 recorded scenarios are unchanged; the Malawi reference remains 2.73x.

0.19.7 — Monthly audit card is independent of chart sizing

  • Monthly Implementation Data no longer inherits the fixed-height component used for charts.
  • The audit section now owns its white background, padding, rounded border, shadow, width, and overflow behaviour.
  • All monthly rows remain available inside the bounded horizontal and vertical scroller, with sticky column headings.
  • A regression now fails if the chart class is put back on the audit table.
  • This completes the F-61 follow-up. Model calculations and all 21 recorded scenarios are unchanged.

0.19.6 — Population need is no longer presented as forecast demand

  • The blue country field is now labelled Rural Population Without Basic Sanitation: an observed measure of need, not forecast borrowers.
  • The reach card now says Share of Unserved Rural Target Reached rather than claiming that the result closes the SDG 6 gap.
  • The methodology states that willingness to borrow, affordability, eligibility, product preference, and monthly uptake are not modelled.
  • Toilet output is therefore conditional delivery potential under sufficient uptake, not an adoption forecast.
  • No national uptake percentage was invented from the Malawi pilot’s unrelated geographic target.
  • F-64 is resolved. All numeric results and all 21 recorded scenarios are unchanged.

0.19.5 — Sustainability scorecard uses canonical metrics

  • Grant Equity Multiple now shows the model’s real ratio in x, rather than a false 0.0% produced by a missing field.
  • Capital Preservation now reports ending net assets against all initial capital; it no longer mixes ring-fenced grant cash into a supposed investor-recovery ratio.
  • OSS and FSS are separately named and defined: OSS covers operations, while FSS also includes write-downs and investor interest.
  • The methodology now explains all five sustainability measures side by side.
  • An unused second summary renderer with conflicting formulas was removed, and a browser-path regression now pins the visible values to the canonical KPIs.
  • F-63 is resolved. Engine calculations and all 21 recorded scenarios are unchanged.

0.19.4 — Model flow and calibration are visible on site

  • The model now links directly to a five-step plain-language guide showing how evidence and assumptions flow through capital, monthly cashflow, delivery, loan cohorts, and results.
  • A calibration checklist identifies what must be replaced with programme evidence before results are used in a funding or policy decision.
  • The guide distinguishes blue dated country evidence from amber programme assumptions, and arithmetic integrity from programme viability.
  • Claims about real-time data, rate-driven demand, double-entry accounting, and guaranteed repayment were removed because the model does not support them.
  • All 41 numeric controls are covered by the canonical parameter dictionary and a new regression keeps that coverage complete.
  • F-62 is resolved. Model calculations and all 21 recorded scenarios are unchanged.

0.19.3 — Monthly audit table stays inside its panel

  • Monthly Implementation Data no longer extends below the white card background.
  • The wide month-level audit table scrolls inside its own bounded panel both horizontally and vertically.
  • Column headings stay visible while scrolling through longer scenarios.
  • F-61 is resolved. Model calculations and all 21 recorded scenarios are unchanged.

0.19.2 — Cross-country and parameter robustness audit

  • The live audit now converts current World Bank observations exactly as the browser does and completes a full model run for every selectable country.
  • All 44 countries currently have the essential observations, pass semantic range checks, complete the model, and preserve every ledger invariant.
  • A permanent deterministic test now covers 384 combinations of capital, duration, demand, rates, defaults, operating costs, delivery capacity, health, carbon, and time-value assumptions.
  • Country-derivation tests verify whole-person target population, rounded growth, GNI-over-GDP fallback, and safe refusal when an essential observation is missing.
  • F-60 is resolved. Inputs, formulas, screen behavior, and all 21 recorded scenarios are unchanged.

0.19.1 — Financial assumptions show relevant API context

  • Household and enterprise loan rates now show the dated country commercial-lending benchmark beside each field, without replacing the programme’s product prices.
  • Malawi therefore retains the illustrative 40% household and 10% enterprise rates while showing 37.1% commercial lending for 2024 as context.
  • Annual fixed operating cost now states that a country macro API cannot infer the programme staffing, office, vehicles, systems, audit, and field-service budget.
  • USD cost escalation now shows local-currency CPI with its date but does not apply it without an exchange-rate and programme cost-basket forecast. Malawi shows 28.4% CPI for 2025 while retaining 3.32% USD escalation.
  • Blue fields are country observations or transparent derivations. Amber fields are programme assumptions that APIs may inform but never set.
  • F-59 is resolved. Model calculations and all 21 recorded scenarios are unchanged.

0.19.0 — Impact estimates are explicit and evidence-linked

  • Jobs Supported now plots correctly. The Malawi reference assumption is an editable three jobs per active enterprise: one owner/operator plus two full-time-equivalent workers.
  • The jobs headline and chart now use the same formula. The old unsupported $3,000-per-job valuation is removed, and the result is not described as job-years.
  • The DALY field is derived conservatively from the WHO lower estimate of sanitation-attributable diarrhoeal burden and matching-year World Bank exposure data. Malawi uses 2019 data: 0.0096719 DALY per exposed person-year.
  • A separate, editable 21% health-effect assumption uses the 2022 meta-analysis point estimate for basic sanitation without sewer connection. This avoids silently treating 100% of the country burden as averted.
  • The five-year Malawi reference now shows about 2,139 physical DALYs averted. Value per DALY remains zero, so SROI stays 2.73 rather than silently monetising the estimate.
  • The derivation is available for 42 of 44 selector countries. Eritrea and Tuvalu fall back clearly to Not estimated because a required source is unavailable.
  • Carbon remains off until the programme supplies a project-specific baseline and technology factor. The result now says Not estimated — project factor required instead of presenting zero as measured mitigation.
  • F-56, F-57, and F-58 are resolved. Financial outputs and all 21 recorded scenarios are unchanged.

0.18.1 — Programme summary fits the page

  • The tables below Programme Summary (End of Project Estimation) now stay inside the page at laptop, tablet, and phone widths.
  • The three cards reflow automatically to two or one column when the main panel is narrower.
  • Long summary labels and values wrap inside their card rather than forcing the page wider.
  • The 22-column monthly audit table remains readable and scrolls horizontally inside its own bounded area.
  • F-55 is resolved. Model calculations and all recorded scenarios are unchanged.

0.18.0 — Enterprise rollout starts as a pilot

  • The Malawi default no longer assumes 285 enterprises are recruited, capitalised, and operating before month one.
  • A new Initial ME Network (% of Target) input starts an explicit pilot, capped by available loan capital. The illustrative 15% default starts 84 of the 560-enterprise target.
  • Month-one enterprise interest falls from about $25,800 to $7,600. The network then grows to about 232 enterprises by month 12 and 314 by month 24.
  • The five-year reference builds 83,350 toilets, fully repays senior principal, has FSS of 1.06, and remains viable. The previous 118,509-toilet result relied on the aggressive instant rollout.
  • A tested 10% pilot had FSS of only 0.995; 15% retains a modest operating margin while remaining far below the old 51% starting network.
  • F-54 is resolved. Stress scenarios are deliberately more conservative and the 21 recorded scenario results have been updated.

0.17.0 — Country-derived inputs are visibly sourced

  • The three model inputs loaded or derived from World Bank observations now share one clear blue background and outline.
  • Target population identifies its two-observation derivation; population growth and income identify their direct observation year. Hovering a blue field shows the source description.
  • Editing a blue field removes the marker, so a user-entered value is not still presented as API-derived.
  • Household, enterprise, and investor interest assumptions are not coloured as country observations; market lending remains dated context only.
  • F-53 is resolved. Model calculations and all reference results are unchanged.

0.16.0 — Evidence-bounded sensitivity analysis

  • A reproducible one-at-a-time sensitivity tool now compares low, base, and high cases for up to eight parameters and ranks them by the selected outcome swing.
  • Every range must include an explicit source, bracket the base value, and pass the same validation as an ordinary model run. Invalid or undocumented ranges are refused.
  • No generic percentage shock, undocumented Malawi range, probability distribution, or live data fetch is built in.
  • Every case reports net assets, ending and minimum cash, toilets, senior repayment, SROI, and viability in deterministic JSON suitable for audit.
  • The base model is unchanged: all 21 recorded scenarios and the standard Malawi result retain exactly the same values.

0.15.0 — Production constraints follow actual output

  • The production-constraint headline now uses the same demand, enterprise-capacity, grant, loan, and final-output quantities as the monthly production calculation.
  • The largest source of foregone monthly output is shown first, with other material causes listed as secondary. Months affected by several causes are labelled Mixed.
  • For the standard Malawi scenario, enterprise capacity is primary; loan and grant capital are secondary. This describes production and does not replace the separate fund-viability verdict.
  • A three-test regression reconciles demand to production and prevents the constraint explanation drifting back to a cash-balance heuristic.
  • No financial, production, reach, impact, viability, or solver number changed. Q14 is resolved and no modelling question remains open.

0.14.0 — Partial-year horizons fully reported

  • Valid horizons shorter than a year now show charts and a labelled reporting period instead of a blank dashboard.
  • Horizons such as 18 months now include the final six months instead of stopping annual views at month 12.
  • Partial periods are labelled clearly, for example Year 2 (6 months).
  • Period flows and cumulative reach are checked against the underlying monthly series, and the 6-month render path is tested.
  • F-52 is resolved. Whole-year scenarios and the standard Malawi result are unchanged.

0.13.0 — Annual grant chart corrected

  • Annual grant spending now always appears as an outflow in the cash-flow chart.
  • The old chart treated separate yearly totals as cumulative. Falling spending could therefore appear as positive grant income, including income in a year when no grant was spent.
  • For the Malawi reference run, the corrected grant bars are approximately -$278k, -$356k, -$292k, $0, and $0.
  • A new render-path check compares the actual chart configuration with the engine's annual grant series.
  • F-51 is resolved. The underlying ledger and headline Malawi result are unchanged.

0.12.0 — Zero capacity is now honored

  • Entering zero for Micro-enterprises / Unit now creates a genuine no-network stress case.
  • The old calculation silently changed zero back to the default of 20, making the adverse scenario look identical to the healthy Malawi reference run.
  • With zero capacity, the model now launches no enterprises, builds no toilets, and reports the resulting senior-debt shortfall instead of a viable verdict.
  • Missing or malformed inputs still stop before calculation; only an explicit, valid zero is preserved.
  • F-50 is resolved. The default Malawi result and all 21 recorded scenarios are unchanged.

0.11.0 — Monthly implementation table corrected

  • Every monthly figure now appears under the correct heading. A missing header previously shifted ME finance, operating-cost, investor-payment, net-cash, and ending-cash values one column to the right.
  • The table now labels New MEs (a count) separately from New ME Loans (a currency amount).
  • New-enterprise counts now use the exact number launched by the simulation instead of dividing the loan amount by setup cost alone.
  • At the current Malawi defaults, the old shortcut overstated launches by 3.8 times because it ignored working capital included in each enterprise loan.
  • F-49 is resolved. Cashflows, headline results, and the five-year Malawi reference result are unchanged.

0.10.0 — Advice is now model-tested

  • The advice button now recalculates the current scenario and shows only changes that were tested by rerunning the model.
  • The old advisor could recommend a longer investor term even where the model showed that it worsened repayment; that duplicate path has been removed.
  • The control is now labelled “Review model-tested advice” rather than implying that its deterministic calculations are artificial intelligence.
  • Recommendations appear beside the viability result and never alter the user’s assumptions.
  • F-48 is resolved. Model outputs and the five-year Malawi reference result are unchanged.

0.9.0 — Scenario exports made self-consistent

  • CSV and clipboard reports now retain the exact assumptions used by the successful calculation, even if the form is edited before export.
  • The copied balance sheet now uses the model's tracked senior liability, including capitalised interest, instead of reconstructing a smaller number.
  • The copied leverage ratio now uses the same definition as the dashboard and canonical KPI.
  • Two new export regressions verify the scenario snapshot, senior liability, and leverage.
  • F-47 is resolved. Model outputs and the five-year Malawi reference result are unchanged.

0.8.0 — Invalid inputs stop before calculation

  • Blank, malformed, infinite, and mathematically invalid inputs now produce no model result instead of allowing NaN, Infinity, a crash, or a plausible-looking answer.
  • The model reports every detected field together so users can correct the scenario in one pass.
  • A failed rerun clears the previous result, preventing an older scenario from being exported under newly entered assumptions.
  • Checks cover mathematical requirements only; the model does not invent maximum prices, rates, capital, or programme scale.
  • F-46 is resolved. The 21 recorded scenarios and five-year Malawi reference result are unchanged.

0.7.0 — Investor debt settlement completed

  • Old capitalised investor interest is now repaid before current interest and original principal.
  • Paying old interest reduces both the arrears balance and the senior liability; the model separately reports total capitalised, later repaid, and still outstanding.
  • At wind-up, positive loan-ledger cash is paid to the senior investor before the ledger freezes. Grant cash remains ring-fenced.
  • Two new integrity checks reconcile the senior balance and prevent a wound-up fund from retaining loan cash while senior debt remains unpaid.
  • F-44 and F-45 are resolved. The five-year Malawi reference result is unchanged.

0.6.0 — Evidence-based defaults and safer country data

  • Malawi now opens with dated population, sanitation-gap, growth, and income observations plus sourced household size, district count, enterprise capital, loan terms, and time-saving assumptions.
  • Country loading changes only observed target population, population growth, and income. CPI, commercial lending, poverty, Gini, and sanitation coverage are shown as dated context; they do not silently set financing, losses, costs, capacity, or subsidy policy.
  • Missing essential country observations clear the affected field and pause automatic calculation instead of retaining another country’s value.
  • A genuine 0% portfolio write-down now remains zero. Demand now grows before current production is subtracted.
  • DALY and carbon assumptions default to zero until programme evidence is supplied.

Known limitations requiring follow-up

  • Long-horizon warning: the five-year Malawi pilot case is viable, but the 20-year stress winds up in month 101 after 86,076 toilets and ends about $11,000 insolvent. This result is disclosed rather than tuned away.
  • Decision-support roadmap: sensitivity is currently a deterministic JSON tool; an on-page sensitivity view and transparent JSON scenario save/load/compare remain to be built.
  • Currency: the model remains explicitly USD-only. It does not apply a spot exchange rate to multi-year cashflows or model exchange-rate risk.
  • Calibration: financing, operating-cost, loss, capacity, contingency, and subsidy assumptions remain illustrative until replaced with programme evidence.

  • 0.4.0 / S4: replaced unsafe monotonic solver assumptions with grid-then-bisect searches and explicit failure results.
  • 0.3.0 / S3: corrected reserves, enterprise capital, terminal wind-up, impact service life, and model mathematics.
  • 0.2.0 / S0–S2: added the safety net, fixed silent input and unit errors, separated integrity from viability, and stopped the controller rewriting user inputs.
